Reusable PP Meat Lugs vs. Disposable Cardboard: A Real ROI Analysis

Meatlugs Editorial • September 2026

The Hidden Cost of Single-Use Cardboard in Meat Processing

For most meat processing operations, cardboard lugs feel like the path of least resistance: low unit cost, familiar workflow, no washing infrastructure required. But when procurement teams dig into the actual numbers, a different picture emerges. Cardboard absorbs moisture, weakens during cold-chain handling, and contributes to recurring disposal costs that compound over time. In a high-throughput facility running hundreds of lug cycles per day, those costs add up faster than most buyers expect.

The real question for any serious food-processing procurement manager isn't "what does a lug cost?" It's "what does a lug cost per trip, over 36 months?" That's where reusable polypropylene (PP) meat lugs consistently outperform their cardboard counterparts.

What Reusable PP Meat Lugs Actually Cost Per Trip

A standard commercial-grade reusable PP meat lug — typically 60 to 80 liters in volume, with a solid PP wall and integrated handles — carries an upfront unit cost in the $18–$45 range depending on size, wall thickness, and order volume. That may look steep compared to a $1.50 cardboard alternative. But reusable PP lugs are rated for 200–400 trip cycles under normal operating conditions with proper cleaning protocols.

Do the math: a $30 PP lug used for 300 trips costs $0.10 per trip. A $1.50 cardboard lug used once costs $1.50 per trip. Even factoring in washing labor, water, and sanitizing chemicals — commonly estimated at $0.03–$0.07 per cycle — reusable PP lugs reduce packaging cost per trip by 85–93% in closed-loop operations.

The PP meat lugs from Elipacko are manufactured from FDA-compliant virgin polypropylene resin and designed specifically for meat processing environments — non-porous, non-absorbent, and rated for temperatures from -20°F to 140°F.

Running the Numbers: A 3-Year ROI Model

Consider a mid-size beef processing facility moving 1,500 lugs per day across two shifts. At cardboard pricing:

  • Daily cardboard spend: 1,500 × $1.50 = $2,250
  • Annual spend: $821,250
  • 3-year total: $2,463,750

Switching to reusable PP at a $30/unit acquisition cost, assuming 300 trips per lug and a 5% annual attrition rate:

  • Fleet size needed: ~7,500 units (5-day rotation buffer)
  • Initial acquisition: $225,000
  • Annual replacement (5% attrition): $11,250
  • Annual washing cost ($0.05/cycle × 1,500 × 300 days): $22,500
  • 3-year total cost: $225,000 + $33,750 + $67,500 = $326,250

3-year savings: over $2.1 million. Even with a conservative attrition rate and higher washing costs, reusable PP delivers a payback period of under 4 months in this scenario.

Beyond the Spreadsheet: Operational and Compliance Gains

Cost per trip is the headline metric, but reusable PP lugs deliver secondary gains that procurement and operations teams increasingly recognize. Cardboard breaks down under moisture and pressure, creating product contamination risk and inconsistent stacking performance on high-speed lines. PP lugs maintain structural integrity across thousands of cycles, reducing line downtime from container failures.

In USDA-inspected facilities, the material properties of PP — non-porous, smooth-walled, easily sanitized — simplify compliance documentation. Inspectors can verify sanitation records and container condition in ways that aren't possible with single-use packaging.
